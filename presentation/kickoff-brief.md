# CTF Kickoff Brief

Source content and speaker notes for the participant opening presentation.
Keep the live delivery to about 5-7 minutes and avoid demonstrating a
challenge solution.

## Goal

Before logging in, participants should know what the event is, how to enter a
released track, where to get help, and how scoring is announced for that event.

## Slide outline

1. **Welcome:** event name, acceptable-use boundary, and learning goals.
2. **Tracks:** Bandit for Linux fundamentals, Krypton for cryptography, and
   Natas for web security. Use the current catalog rather than a fixed level
   count.
3. **How the event runs:** CTFd is the place to log in, read challenges, launch
   environments, submit flags, and view scoreboards.
4. **Start Here:** wait for the announced track release, open its Start Here
   challenge, and follow its current connection instructions.
5. **Lab boundary:** use only the assigned range environment. Do not probe
   other participants, event infrastructure, or external systems.
6. **Hints and help:** explain the published hint policy and point participants
   to instructors when an environment appears broken.
7. **Scoring:** describe only the award and tie-break rules approved for this
   event. If staged scoreboards are enabled, each game has its own start time
   and scoreboard.
8. **Breaks and states:** an announced break does not necessarily stop scoring;
   staff will say whether a stage remains active, its scoreboard is hidden, or
   scoring is locked.
9. **Start:** direct participants to the announced track's Start Here challenge.

## Presenter notes

- Do not promise exact release times or award structures before approval.
- Do not demonstrate commands or routes that duplicate the learning objective
  of a challenge.
- Use the current `docs/participant-quickstart.md` for track-specific launch
  instructions, especially the Natas attacker-workstation flow.
